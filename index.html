So, you want to catch the ISS?
Here are few steps that will help you:

- Search for "ISS flyover *your country*"
In my country there's space influencer who keeps us updated on ISS flights. But don't worry if you don't know anyone like this! I'm pretty sure that if you look up on google or somewhere this sentence, you'll easily find a website that will show you when and where (north, south..) you can see the ISS in YOUR country! And, by the way, I'm currently working on a website like this for COUNTRIES ALL OVER THE WORLD! The project is called ISS HUNT, so if you are interested check it out and let me know what country shall I add first!

- Look for bright and pretty fast point
Now, when you know when the ISS is flying over, just go out at this particular time and look at the night sky! You should notice bright, small point moving fast through the sky - that's the ISS! The flights last between 3-7 minutes so you can admire the spacecraft without getting bored.

HOW TO CATCH STARLINK - GUIDE

So, you want to catch Starlink?
Here are few steps that will help you:

Search for "Starlink tracker (*your country*)"
In your browser search for "starlink tracker" or "starlink tracker *your country*". I like to use this tracker where you can pick your country AND city! The website also provides a live map, where you can see the positions of the Starlink satellites!!

And now, when you know when it's flying over, just go outside and look for long, dashed line crossing the sky. That's the Starlink! It looks this way because it's a constellation of satellites.
In January 2025 there were 6,800 Starlink satellites in orbit, 12,000 satellites is the planned size of the constellation, and there is a possibility of later expansion to 32,000!

Did you know?
Astronomers have expressed concerns regarding the impact of the Starlink constellation on ground-based observations. SpaceX has attempted to address concerns about astronomical interference by reducing the satellites brightness through a reduction in reflected light.
Source: Wikipedia

Congrats!
Now, you know how to spot starlink satellites! Enjoy the flyover!

James Webb Space Telescope

Basic information:
1. Built between 2007 - 2021 by Northrop Grumman, Ball Aerospace & Technologies
2. Insertion into orbit: 25.12.2021, 1:20 PM CET
3. Conducts infrared observations

Wait, infrared observations? What does it mean??????
Let's look at how does James Webb Space Telescope look:

Do you see the gold, honey-like thing? That's Webbs mirror. It has a gold coating to provide infrared reflectivity. The mirror is 6.5-meter-diameter (21-foot) and it's made up of 18 separate hexagonal mirrors.


Webb's Primary Mirror Segments Going into Cryogenic Testing in 2011.
Credits: NASA, David Higginbotham (NASA-MSFC)

The infrared is really usefull for several reasons:
- High-redshift (very early and distant) objects have their visible emissions shifted into the infrared, and therefore their light can be observed only via infrared astronomy
- Infrared light passes more easily through dust clouds than visible light
- Colder objects such as debris disks and planets emit most strongly in the infrared

Mission goals
1. To search for light from the first stars and galaxies that formed in the universe after the Big Bang
2. To study galaxy formation and evolution
3. To understand star formation and planet formation
4. To study planetary systems and the origins of life

Hubble Space Telescope

Basic information:
1. Launch date: April 24 1990, 12:33:51 UTC
2. Named after astronomer Edwin Hubble
3. Built in the 1970s by NASA and ESA

The HST is one of the few telescopes that observes in ultraviolet light.
Besides ultraviolet, his main five instruments conduct observations in visible, and near-infrared regions of the electromagnetic spectrum

Hubble is the only space telescope designed to be maintained in space by astronauts. There were five Space Shuttle missions that repaired and upgraded the telescope. The fifth mission was canceled due to Columbia Disaster. Fortunately, NASA administrator Michael D. Griffin approved it, and the mission completed successfully in 2009.

Astronaut Kathryn Thornton works on Hubble during Servicing Mission 1
Credits: NASA


Let's look at how does Hubble Space Telescope look:

Hubble accommodates five science instruments at a given time (and FGS). Since the final mission in 2009 Hubble has installed:
- Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS, since 2002)
- Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS, since 2009)
- Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS, since 1997, with a break between 2004-2009)
- Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3, since 2009) yes, there were WFC2 and WFC
- Near Infrared Camera and Multi-Object Spectrometer (NICMOS, since 1997, hibernating since 2008)
And the FGS: Fine Guidance Sensors, which are mainly used for aiming the telescope. Since the beginning of the mission. That's and og!

Some of the previous instruments are displayed in the museums. If you want to know more about them check this wikipedia list of HST instruments

Artemis Mission

✶ Artemis I ✶ Artemis II ✶ Artemis III

Artemis - what do you need to know?

The main goal of this mission is to return humans to the Moon after 5 decades, build a moon base and therefore, explore more of the moon.

A brief summary of all Artemis missions:
✶ Artemis I - uncrewed, testing mission (completed)
✶ Artemis II - inspiring and memorable crew, orbiting the Moon and seeing its "invisible" side (completed)
✶ Artemis III - testing mission, but this time with crew (planned for 2027)
✶ Artemis IV - moon landing mission! (planned for early 2028)
✶ Artemis V - another moon landing mission, this time, with lots of experiments and preparations for the construction of a moon base! (planned for late 2028)

How is the spacecraft, that's flying to the moon built?
There are 2 main segments: the SLS Rocket (Space Launch System) and Orion, the Capsule. Below, you can see the whole spacecraft, but we are going to focus on these two main elements.


you can change between the photos by clicking on the circles!

SLS - Space Launch System
It's the most powerful and capable rocket NASA has ever built! It's designed to send Orion, astronauts and other supplies to the Moon in a single mission - SLS is the only launch vehicle that can make it possible! In the future, this rocket will be able to send increasingly heavy payloads to the Moon with even greater power!

Orion
Orion is the racket capsule, a place where the crew lives. It is the only capsule that is capable of taking astronauts that far. Every other one fly only in Earth orbit. The Artemis II crew named the capsule "Integrity", what do you think - how will future missions name Orion??

Did you know?
The name "Artemis" comes from Artemis, the goddess of the moon, who's a twin sister of Apollo (the god of the sun and light).

Artemis 1 mission

Artemis 1 mission was an uncrewed, lunar orbital flight test.
It tested the operation of the systems, checked any potential errors and looked for things that could go wrong.

There were three astronaut-like mannequins in the Orion spacecraft. Besides them, Orion carried a Shaun the Sheep toy, representing the ESA's contribution to the mission and a plushie of Snoopy! The Snoopy was a zero gravity indicator: because of no crew, he was a visual signal, when the spacecraft has reached the weightlessness of microgravity

This mission lasted 25 days (from November 16 to December 11 of 2022). Artemis I orbited the Moon, spent a few days in orbit, and then returned to Earth. You can see it's route below:



if you have struggle with reading, move you cursor on the map to magnify!

Artemis 2 mission

Artemis 2 did not land on the Moon; it was a flyby, testing mission but this time with crew. During this mission, humans traveled further from Earth than ever before in history, reaching the far side of the Moon which is invisible from Earth. The previous record was roughly 400 171 km (248 655 miles), set by the Apollo 13 mission (which - as a fun fact - reached that distance due to a breakdown).
You can see it's route below:



if you have struggle with reading, move you cursor on the map to magnify!

Artemis 2 only circled the Moon without entering orbit. Thanks to this, it returned to Earth automatically along its flight trajectory, without any additional maneuver.

The Artemis II crew was really inspiring to the young generation. They made kids dream big again and believe in themselfes! The Artemis II team named Orion "Integrity" - it's their's mission capsule nick!

Artemis 3 mission

Initially, this mission was intended to be a moon landing mission. Instead, Artemis 3 will fly to low Earth orbit, where it will conduct tests including rendezvous and docking capabilities.

"While this is a mission to Earth orbit, it is an important stepping stone to successfully landing on the Moon with Artemis IV. Artemis III is one of the most highly complex missions NASA has undertaken" - Jeremy Parsons


NASA moving the core stage, or the largest section, of the SLS (Space Launch System) rocket that will launch the crewed Artemis III mission in 2027. Source: NASA, Credits: Eric Bordelon

Learn more about the Artemis III mission here

What about the Artemis IV and V?

The Artemis IV mission is planned for early 2028.

"Artemis IV will be one of the most complex undertakings of engineering and human ingenuity in the history of deep space exploration, exploring the lunar South Pole region. The astronauts' observations, samples, and data collected will expand our understanding of our solar system and home planet, while inspiring the Artemis Generation.

Artemis IV astronauts will travel to lunar orbit, where two crew members will descend to the surface and spend approximately a week near the South Pole of the Moon conducting new science before returning to lunar orbit to join their crew for the journey back to Earth."

Source: NASA

The Artemis IV crew will reach the lunar orbit aboard the Orion Spacecraft and then it will dock with a lander in preparation for their journey to the moon surface! Orion is an extraordinary feat of humanity - it is the only spacecraft capable of bringing the crew back to Earth at the speeds associated with a return from the vicinity of the Moon.

Orion really is one of the best spaecrafts ever!!

Voyager Spacecrafts

Voyager 1 and 2 are twins - They were launched 2 weeks apart in 1977, they're built the same way and yet they have way different stories. One made a grand tour of the solar system's outer planets, and one is the most distant spacecraft ever!

They both carry a copy of the Golden Record (you'll find some photos from golden record in "Voyager Photos" App on the left). They also both have:
1. a radio dish with a diameter of 3.7 meters (it transmits data to Earth)
2. a set of 16 thrusters (they point these dishes towards Earth and control the spacecrafts orientation)
3. 11 scientific instruments

On this photo you can see Voyager's 11 instruments and other parts like the High-Gain Antenna.

Instruments highlighted by light yellow are off on Voyager 2.

Instruments highlighted by aqua are off on Voyager 1.


Image Credits: NASA/JPL-Caltech


These are the only operational spacecrafts in interstellar space that are still sending back measurements of the interstellar medium! The spacecrafts were launched into space during a rare alignment of the gas giant planets that occurs once every 176 years. The timing was perfect!

Vojager 2
Launched first, the only one to reach Uran and Neptune! Thanks to it, humanity gained a wealth of information about these planets - information we still use today.

Vojager 1
It was launched 2 weeks after Voyager 2, but it's the one that is the most distant from Earth human-made object and it overtook Voyager 2 in just a few months! How did that happend? Because it traveled at a higher speed on a more direct trajectory.

Only Voyager 2 reached Uranus and Neptune. Voyager 1 (thanks to his trajectory) was able to visit one of Saturn's moons - Titan. Unfortunately, this visit made visiting Uranus and Neptune impossible.

Twin Rovers

Opportunity

1. Launched on 7 July 2003
2. Rocket: Delta II Heavy
3. Last signal: June 2018
4. Landed on mars: 24 January 2004

Spirit

1. Launched on 10 June 2003
2. Rocket: Delta II
3. Mission end: 25 May 2011
4. Landed on mars: 3 January 2004

The mission duration was planned for ~92 days but both rovers lasted years!! Opportunity stopped responding in 2018, due to a dust storm, and Spirit ended its mission in 2011. Below, you can quickly check the mission statistics:



Opportunity, also called Oppy or MER-B (Mars Exploration Rover - B) was launched by a Delta II "Heavy" rocket, the traditional Delta II launched Oppy's twin - Spirit (MER-A)


Few of Many Discoveries

Opportunity found clay minerals formed in neutral-pH water, which indicates that there was once water on Mars.

It was a crater explorer. The Endeavour Crater has a diameter of about 14 miles (22 kilometers). The yellow line shows a part of Opportunity's route, a marathon distance, travelled since its landing to 2015.

Opportunity had also discovered small deposits of hematite, veins of gypsum and many more things, indicating the past presence of water on Mars. Oppy's selfie on the left!

Spirit discovered unusual rocks that were 10 times richer in key chemicals. Such rocks formed on Mars in a wet and warm environment that could have been conducive to the development of life.

At "Home Plate" (the rover's final resting place) Spirit discovered 90% pure silica and an ancient volcano eruption. Conditions of these discoveries can support microbial life On Eath. What do you think? Do the same applies on Mars?
